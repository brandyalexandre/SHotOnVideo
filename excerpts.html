The Prologue

Chapter “When Did You Discover Sex?”

I don’t think that’s something with a quick and easy answer. My discovery of sex can’t be reduced to a simple origin story—something people reach for to find an “aha!” moment like some kind of X-rated Bat-Signal summoning Brandy Alexandre. There was no single turning point from innocence to experience—only layers that built on each other over time. For me, some of those early layers involved exposure to sex that made it feel intriguing rather than taboo, even in circumstances that would leave most people aghast—horrified, even. Today some might call it grooming.

“For me, some of those early layers involved exposure to sex that made it feel intriguing rather than taboo.”

Sex Scenes

Chapter "What Was it Like on the Set?"

Unfortunately, on the movie Rock ’n’ Roll Heaven, John Stagliano hired Jeff “Woop” Warner—guitarist from the band Black ‘n Blue—to have an authentic rock star do hardcore. But Woop refused to embrace the norms of sex work. I didn’t like him from the start—he was acting like a caricature of a spoiled Hollywood star who expected everyone to cater to his whims. I was willing to deal with it to a point, but I drew the line when he refused to shower before our sex scene. Woop essentially said, “I’m a rocker and this is the way I am. I wouldn’t shower for any chick wanting to f*** me so there’s no reason I should now.” The point he missed was that I wasn’t a fan. I didn’t want to f*** him—I was hired to. But that didn’t mean I had to, so I refused to do the scene.

“I didn’t want to f*** him—I was hired to. But that didn’t mean I had to, so I refused to do the scene.”

Finding Someone to Love

Chapter "Was It Hard to Have a Relationship?"

As the conversation made a sharp turn, I found out Jack had told Barry and Linda about me a long time ago. They were probably wondering when I was going to bring it up so they could ask questions. I was making an effort not to bring it up, trying not to say something porn-adjacent because I spoke openly about it with Jack. But once it was out in the open, the usual questions arose: How many movies did you make? Did you make a lot of money? What about AIDS? I answered them by rote and the evening became less like a dinner party and more like a personal appearance. I found myself in “Brandy Mode,” minding my public manners, consciously giving equal attention to each person, especially Linda, because the guys were nearly 100% focused on me, and having absolutely no fun whatsoever.

“I found myself in ‘Brandy Mode,’ ... consciously giving equal attention to each person, ... and having absolutely no fun whatsoever.”

Best Butt(e) in the West

Chapter "Why Did You Quit Directing?"

The sign was easy to find, but I was having a terrible time trying to record it while driving past it. A few minutes later, one of the guys from the store passed by on his way home (yeah, right) and offered to help. He drove while I hung out the window trying to record the sign, but still without much luck. It was a small sign on a tall post that was too high to get a good angle. He got on his car phone (a novelty at the time) and called his buddy with a truck. That guy showed up with a few more friends, and I stood in the bed of the truck with a guy on either side to steady me and we got the shot.

“He got on his car phone (a novelty at the time) and called his buddy with a truck.”

Personal Appearances

Chapter "Did You Ever Hang Out with Fans?"

The dance circuit exploded in the late ’80s, and porn stars were headlining as strippers all around the country. The featured porn stars often had incredible costumes and big productions, like Hyapatia Lee’s fire dance or Selena Steele’s gymnastics. I’m sure the shows varied with the performers, but I always declined, knowing I would be a flop. I was not a stripper—I can’t even dance. I actually tried it once: Viper coaxed me into doing amateur night at The Body Shop on Sunset Blvd. The polite applause I got for stumbling around to Roxette’s 'The Look' was probably out of pity. I certainly didn’t win anything. I just couldn’t see myself doing live performance—I left that behind in third grade.

“The polite applause I got for stumbling around to Roxette’s “The Look” was probably out of pity.”
